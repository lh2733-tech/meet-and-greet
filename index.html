Meet & Greet Reviewer — Master Specification

Version: v1.0
Effective date: March 2026

This page defines the authoritative rules for evaluating Meet & Greet presentations. It governs baseline requirements, scoring rubric, formatting rules, severity logic, readiness scoring, and required output structure.

1) Meet & Greet Deck Definition & Section Map

Evaluate slides by content, not titles.

  1. Cover / Title Slide
  2. Agenda
  3. Company Overview
  4. Organizational Structure / Team
  5. Corporate History / Milestones
  6. Corporate Facts & Safety
  7. Strengths / Value Proposition
  8. Our Clients
  9. Applicable Projects (Overview)
  10. Applicable Projects (Case Studies)
  11. Closing / Contact

Common Synonym Mapping:

  • Company Overview → Who We Are, About Us, At a Glance
  • Organizational Structure → Team, Leadership, Org Chart
  • Corporate History → Timeline, Milestones
  • Corporate Facts & Safety → Safety, Risk & Compliance
  • Strengths → Differentiators, Why Us
  • Applicable Projects → Relevant Experience, Portfolio
  • Closing → Thank You, Contact

2) Baseline Checklist (Inclusion Criteria)

Mark each as: ✅ Included, ⚠️ Partially Included, ❌ Missing

Cover / Title Slide

  • Company slogan
  • Company name
  • Date of presentation
  • Firm you are presenting to

Agenda

  • Agenda slide included
  • Company Overview listed
  • Relevant Experience listed
  • Q&A listed

Company Overview

  • Overview paragraph included
  • Primary services described
  • Geographic reach noted

Organizational Structure

  • Organizational chart included
  • Employee counts listed
  • Licenses listed
  • Years in business stated

Corporate History

  • History narrative included
  • Certifications listed

Corporate Facts & Safety

  • 2–3 similar jobs listed
  • Trades self-performed listed
  • EMR ratings (up to 3 years) included

Strengths

  • 3–4 differentiators listed

Our Clients

  • Prominent clients listed
  • Clients similar to Columbia highlighted

Applicable Projects

  • Minimum of 4 projects included
  • Dates, values, scope listed
  • Photos included

Closing

  • Contact information included
  • Thank you / closing slide included

  2. Company Overview

4a) Rubric Scoring Examples (Norming Guidance)

These examples standardize what qualifies as Poor (1), Good (3), or Great (5).

1. Cover & Context

  • 1 – Poor: Generic title
    Example: “Company Presentation”
  • 3 – Good: Company + audience + date
    Example: “ABC Construction — Presented to Columbia University — March 2025”
  • 5 – Great: Contextualized to institution & meeting
    Example: “ABC Construction — Meet & Greet — Columbia Facilities — March 14, 2025”

2. Company Overview

  • 1: Generic marketing
    Example: “We are a full-service construction company.”
  • 3: Services & markets stated
    Example: “Founded 2019, specializing in interior renovations.”
  • 5: Tied to institutional needs
    Example: “Renovations in occupied institutional buildings.”

3. Org Structure / Team

  • 1: Roles unclear
  • 3: Roles & names listed
  • 5: Roles tied to credentials and accountability

4. Corporate History

  • 1: Vague narrative
  • 3: Timeline provided
  • 5: Milestones tied to growth, certification, complexity

5. Corporate Facts & Safety

  • 1: No safety info
  • 3: EMR or basic stats
  • 5: Practices + performance

6. Clients

  • 1: “Various clients”
  • 3: Named clients
  • 5: Clients relevant to institutional scale

7. Applicable Projects

  • 1: Photos only
  • 3: Name, value, scope
  • 5: Mini case studies with scope, scale, duration, role clarity

8. Value Proposition

  • 1: Buzzwords
  • 3: Clear strengths
  • 5: Strengths supported by content

9. Closing

  • 1: Missing or vague
  • 3: Contact info present
  • 5: Clear call to action

5) Scoring Table — Output Contract

Use this exact table structure:

Section | Score (1/3/5) | Rationale (why) | Improvement (what to change) | Starting‑Point Copy (paste‑ready)

Compute the final average as a simple mean of nine scores.
